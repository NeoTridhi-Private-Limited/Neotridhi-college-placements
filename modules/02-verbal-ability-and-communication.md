## Preview: Para jumbles

### The idea in plain words

Think of booking a train ticket on IRCTC. The steps have a natural order: log in, choose the stations and date, pick a train, enter
passenger details, pay, get the ticket. If someone gave you these steps mixed up, you would still sort them, because each step
**depends** on the one before it.

Para jumbles work the same way. Every sentence has clues that show what comes **before** it and what comes **after** it.

### Solved examples

**Example 3 (medium, articles and pronouns).**

- A. The engineer found that a small pipe was leaking.
- B. A factory in Visakhapatnam was losing water every day.
- C. Once it was repaired, the water bill fell by half.
- D. The manager asked an engineer to check the plant.

- B uses "A factory" (first mention). Opener: **B**.
- D: "The manager" (of the factory) asks "an engineer" (first mention of engineer). So D comes after B.
- A: "The engineer" (already mentioned) found the leak. So A comes after D.
- C: "it" = the pipe, so C comes after A.
- **Answer: BDAC.**

**Example 4 (medium, connector).**

- A. However, many of them do not practise speaking.
- B. Most students know English grammar well.
- C. As a result, they hesitate in interviews.
- D. Daily speaking practice can fix this quickly.

- B is the opener (general statement).
- A starts with "However" and "them": it contrasts with B and refers to "students". So B → A.
- C "As a result" gives the result of A.
- D offers the solution, "this" = the hesitation. Last.
- **Answer: BACD.**

### Try 3 questions (solutions open)

**1.**

- A. The dog barked loudly.
- B. A thief tried to enter the house at night.
- C. He ran away when the lights came on.
- D. Its owner woke up and switched on the lights.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**BADC.** B opens with "A thief" (first mention). The dog barked (A). "Its owner" (the dog's) woke up (D). "He" (the thief) ran away (C).

</details>

**2.**

- A. These limits test speed, not only knowledge.
- B. Most campus aptitude tests have strict time limits.
- C. Therefore, practising with a timer is essential.
- D. Students often know the method but run out of time.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**BADC.** B general fact. A "These limits" refers to the time limits in B. D explains the problem. C "Therefore" gives the solution.

</details>

**3.**

- A. But the city did not have enough buses.
- B. Its population doubled in twenty years.
- C. As a result, people bought more two-wheelers.
- D. Hyderabad grew rapidly as an IT hub.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**DBAC.** D names Hyderabad. B "Its population". A "But ... not enough buses". C "As a result".

</details>
